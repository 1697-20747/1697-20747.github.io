The story of Occam's Razor and how the principle of simplicity drove scientific revolutions from Copernicus to Einstein to quantum mechanics. Beautifully written.

## Observations

This is a masterclass in thinking and the foundation of science. Learn who William of Ockham was. Get on an aircraft and land where you were supposed to? Thank Johannes Kepler. The church was of the opinion that the sun revolved around the earth — it had to be simple, right? Churches cannot be wrong after all. Wrong. Thankfully Johannes chose not to oversimplify and fail. He chased that marginal error and dug out the truth.

Life is complicated, sometimes very. Complex and complicated are not the same thing. Sure, reduce and simplify where it makes sense. Oversimplify and you will fail. Want one simple rule to manage the economy by? Bad luck, you can't have it.

The book explores how simplification as a concept is a noble, even essential tool for navigating a complex world. But the real lesson is that oversimplification — when critical details are omitted for convenience or to relieve cognitive dissonance — obscures the core underlying truth.

If you are fortunate enough to have seen *The Crucible*, there is the moment in Act 4 when Judge Danforth refuses to postpone the upcoming hangings of John Proctor and Rebecca Nurse, arguing that because twelve people have already been executed for the same crime, it would be unjust and weak to spare the others. In other words: the situation was simple, I can't be wrong, and if I killed those people for nothing, I would logically have to be wrong. So your innocence must be subordinate to my inability to be wrong. Simply abhorrent.
