How to think with data rather than be misled by it. Cambridge statistician Spiegelhalter walks through the essential principles of statistical reasoning using real-world cases — serial killers, cancer screening, hospital survival rates — to show where statistics illuminate and where they deceive. Less a technical manual than a guide to asking the right questions before trusting any number you are given.

## Observations

This is a great book, as a read and as a reference text. If you are into machine learning, the concepts are essential. If you can find his London School of Economics lecture online it is definitely worth your time. I go back to this one quite often to help clarify my thinking.

This book is about clarity of thinking within the domain of statistics, more than just statistics. Top tip: probably no such thing as probability theory. Pun intended.

If you are making decisions that are important and based on data analysis — stop, pick up this book, reflect, then decide with a lot more care than you were perhaps planning on. Signal, noise, both? Clarity is hard to find. The more you have of one aspect, the more you have to give up on another.
