## Overview

The most important thing to understand about the current moment is not what is changing. It is *how fast* things are changing, and whether that rate of change is itself changing.

It is.

The two tables below are worth sitting with for a moment. The first shows the duration of human technological eras. The second shows how long it took transformative technologies to reach 100 million users. Both tell the same story from different angles. Yes its AI generated as I am too lazy too research them, but we need neither accuracy nor precision here. The time of era change is condensing is the key point.

---

## The Compression of Eras

| Era | Approximate Dates | Duration |
|------|------|------|
| Stone Age | 3.3 million BCE – 3300 BCE | ~3 million years |
| Bronze Age | 3300 BCE – 1200 BCE | ~2,100 years |
| Iron Age | 1200 BCE – 500 CE | ~1,700 years |
| Middle Ages | 500 – 1500 CE | ~1,000 years |
| Early Modern Age | 1500 – 1760 | ~260 years |
| Industrial Age | 1760 – 1970 | ~210 years |
| Information Age | 1970 – 2022 | ~52 years |
| AI Age | 2022 – Present | ~4 years and counting |

The pattern is not subtle. Each era is roughly an order of magnitude shorter than the one before it. The Stone Age lasted three million years. The AI Age has lasted four years and is already reshaping the structure of entire industries.

The question worth asking is not whether this matters. It does. The question is whether human institutions — legal systems, educational structures, regulatory frameworks, political cycles — are capable of adapting at anything approaching this speed. The historical answer is no. Whether this time is different remains to be seen.

## The Acceleration of Adoption

| Technology | Time to Reach ~100 Million Users |
|------------|----------------------------------|
| Telephone | ~75 years |
| Radio | ~38 years |
| Television | ~13 years |
| Internet | ~7 years |
| Facebook | ~4.5 years |
| TikTok | ~9 months |
| ChatGPT | ~2 months |

Two months. From zero to one hundred million users in two months.

For context, it took the telephone seventy-five years to achieve the same milestone. That is not a linear compression. It is something closer to a phase transition — a change in the fundamental nature of the system, not just its speed.

---

## What This Means

The compressing duration of eras and the accelerating adoption of technology are two expressions of the same underlying dynamic. The productive half-life of a skill, a business model, or an institution is shrinking. What worked for a generation now works for a decade. What worked for a decade now works for a few years.

This is not inherently good or bad. It is simply the environment. The relevant question is adaptive capacity — at the individual, organisational, and societal level.

The Stone Age lasted long enough that a skill learned at birth remained useful at death, for tens of thousands of generations in a row. We are now in an era where a skill learned at thirty may be obsolete at forty-five. That is a fundamentally different set of demands on human cognition, institutions, and identity. Did anyone alive at the time really go 'ooh ar, we just moved through an era, did we?'. I suspect there was no realization at the time.

The data above does not tell us what to do about this. But it does make the nature of the problem harder to ignore.

I am old, I slogged through the bits of coding that were software and syntax drive. Every tried to de bug fortran or early java? What a pain. But you did learn the subject matter for all the agony, and there was the perverse joy of when it actually ran. This was the case for years, even as the IDE got better.

Now, I organize my thoughts and in the main operate in CLI, generally making lego bricks of functions to put together. Its back to the IDE for problems or to check the logic, or to run test components. But it is a lot less time in IDE.

Of late, its all about the domain, and the broad structure of what I am trying to do. I don't really do the whole AI one shot prompt thing, as its rubbish in my view. Its not the the AI is rubbish, its that you have no idea what you want so it creates some kind of nonsense. Then I run almost everything in stand alone bash scripts. But I don't do front ends, so who cares about that.

So.... its not like what I learnt was wasted, quite the opposite. But I don't approach the task in the way I used to at all. That pace of change has been fast. Have you really tried to get the transformer architecture in your head? To the point where you know the flaws? Not so easy.

Change is accelerating, beyond our personal and institutional ability to cope. But I get to live through one of the great transitions. Good or bad, its a wildly interesting time to be alive to see it. 

> The future is not longer mine to do, only to bear witness to and understand. Its yours. So don't be assholes and f**k it up for the rest of us!
