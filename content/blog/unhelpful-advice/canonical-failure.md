>  “…....if that process goes wrong - if the implementation of a burst of investment opportunities in some sectors is not matched by a holding back of investment in other sectors and by restraint in current consumption by households and the government - too much spending will be brought forward from the future. Future spending will be insufficient to rake up all the output of the newly installed capital when the investment boom has subsided. " - Bernard Connolly. Source: Authors: Bernard Connolly Year Published: 2023 Country of Publication: United Kingdom Format: Hardback ISBN: 9781911397410 Number of Pages: 528 Publication Date: 07/09/2023. Page 20.

> "But by contending that cyclical disturbances are self-correcting, it has completely forgotten the understanding, however incomplete, painfully acquired by economists in the very different academic conditions of the 1920s and 1930s: cyclical disturbances are not self-correcting once monetary policy has gone wrong. An important consequence of the canonical model's catastrophic failure to recognise the importance of swings in the balance of aggregate supply and aggregate demand was that the industry and most central bankers - and the financial sector - believed that a deep recession (other than one like the Volcker recession of the early 1980s in the US, created, whether deliberately or accidentally, by the central bank to reverse an episode of out-of-control inflation) was impossible, or at least unforeseeable until it was too late. The behaviour thereby induced in the financial system - behaviour rightly anathematised by Macintyre - was such that if a recession did in fact occur or came to be seen as probable, many supposedly 'riskless' bets would go wrong and financial chaos could result."
