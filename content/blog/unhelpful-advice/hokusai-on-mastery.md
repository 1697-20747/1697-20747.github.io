> From the age of six I had a mania for drawing the forms of things. By the time I was fifty I had published a universe of designs, but all that I have produced before the age of seventy is not worth taking into account.
>
> At seventy-three I partially understood the structure of birds, animals, insects, and fishes, and the life of grasses and plants.
>
> Therefore, at eighty-six I shall have progressed further; at ninety I shall penetrate even further their secret meaning, and at one hundred I may perhaps truly have reached the level of the marvelous and divine.
>
> If Heaven grants me ten more years, or even five more years, I may become a real painter.
>
> — Katsushika Hokusai, 1835
