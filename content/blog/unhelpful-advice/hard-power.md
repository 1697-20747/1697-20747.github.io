> “Before you step out of that circle give me a reply to lay before the senate” - Gaius Popillius Laenas, Consul of Rome

Roman consul, Gaius Popillius Laenas drew the veritable line in the sand. That line being a circle. It was circa 168 BCE, and Antiochus IV Epiphanes was advancing on Alexandria, threatening to conquer Egypt. Rome was not impressed. One single man, with a small retinue, standing in front of an entire army, delivered quite the message. Antiochus IV pondered, and caved to the credible threat of Rome. He placed in his hand the Roman decree, or senatus-consultum ordering him to put an end at once to the war with Ptolemy, before stating the ultimatum as above. Source: Polybius, Greek historian. He was a contemporary of the time.

The classic authoritative statement of hard power. Not to worry, the threat of even more incremental regulation from the EU shall make the evil doers of the world tremble at the thought. Or perhaps not.... Though we should still fear the concept of power above all.
