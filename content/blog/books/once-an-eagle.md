A sweeping military novel following two officers across decades of American wars. One of the finest studies of leadership, character, and moral compromise ever written. Required reading at several US war colleges for good reason.

## Observations

If you have ever been in the army, or have suffered the usual tyranny in a large financial institution (yes large Banks, I do mean you), this one is for you. This is a military classic, that transcends the domain into life. Are you Sam? Really? There are no untainted heroes here. This is the life lesson. The title comes from the fable: "So in the Libyan fable it is told / That once an eagle, stricken with a dart, / Said, when he saw the fashion of the shaft, / 'With our own feathers, not by others' hands, / Are we now smitten'". Read this book for a good history of war. Re-read it as many times as you need to until you understand the enemy is you. All of our carts bounce around in the rutted tracks of our deep rooted tendencies that we laid down long ago to get where we are.
