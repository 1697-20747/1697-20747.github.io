Paul Volcker's memoir and reflection on public service, centered on his fight for "sound money" — stable currency, low inflation, and financial integrity — as a foundation for good government. Volcker is best known for his tenure as Chairman of the Federal Reserve (1979–1987), where he aggressively raised interest rates to break the back of 1970s inflation. The policy caused a deep recession but ultimately restored price stability and credibility to U.S. monetary policy.

## Observations

This is worth your time, especially if you are not from a Latam country and don't know what inflation really is. The hint there is that inflation in the UK over 2020 to 2026 start to finish was about 34%, across the whole period. Enough to sting, as well as enough to popularise the phrase 'cost of living crisis'. Try 19% interest rates on Treasuries, with credit margin on top. Volcker saved us from ourselves. We forget the lesson.

There are many great little vignettes in this book. He makes the case for why the Fed, and all central banks, must retain a core level of independence. This is a well written text from a giant of his time. A soft-spoken actual giant. Who now would ever dare raise rates enough to break the inflation cycle? The political class would revolt. Every president ever elected wants lower rates, more money, and a bigger deficit. Humility does not get you a presidency. Some will do more than others to lash out at the constraint. In difficult times, this is a herculean task. Look to Türkiye, where the abuse of power and degrading of the independence of the central bank has resulted in the inevitable, horrific and systemic inflation.

Listen to the man in his own words — there are various in-person interviews online. He gave to the end, finishing his book for future generations. His comments on the malaise of government in the US, made back in 2013, are prophetic for the challenges of 2026. Articulate, thoughtful, considerate and informed. Principled. Perhaps the last of a generation.
