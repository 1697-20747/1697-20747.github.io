Sebastian Mallaby — the same writer who gave us the best account of the hedge fund industry in *More Money Than God* — turns his attention to Demis Hassabis and DeepMind. Mallaby had three years of unprecedented access, which in practice means Hassabis wanted this book written. That is itself an interesting data point.

Hassabis is an unusual character by any measure. Chess prodigy at five, turned down a seven figure offer before eighteen to read computer science at Cambridge, then added a neuroscience PhD on top. Founded DeepMind with a single stated goal — artificial general intelligence — long before the current hysteria made that a fashionable thing to say. Won the Nobel Prize in 2024 for AlphaFold, which solved protein structure prediction and is probably the most practically significant scientific result of the last decade.

The book is not a Silicon Valley story, which is to its credit. Hassabis takes Google's money but remains openly contemptuous of much of the Valley's culture and its leaders. That tension — the idealist inside the machine — runs through the whole narrative.

What makes this worth reading is Mallaby's framing. This is a book about intelligence itself: what it is, how it works, whether you can build it, and what happens if you do. Hassabis is the vehicle for those questions. Whether you find the answers satisfying will depend largely on how seriously you take the AGI thesis. I take it seriously enough to find this uncomfortable reading in places, which is probably the right reaction.

Read alongside *Determined* by Sapolsky for a useful corrective on what intelligence actually is.

I was working on repeating the famous Atari machine learning project from scratch. I was aware of it, and was trying to DIY it from scratch. Not so easy as it turns out. Well, if I had managed it in 2016, I'd be up $150m. I didn't so bad luck. But the book covers the back story of this in detail. I am glad I read this book just for the impact on that little project. They were geniuses, and had a lot of compute. I have neither. I feel less bad about my simplified version now.

*Key Observations:*
- Its a great snapshot of a key moment in time. In decades to come this will be a reference text.
- Like it or not, we live in an era of upheaval. Perhaps more so than we can appreciate in the moment.
- It is deep comp science light, so it is more readable. But the concepts are laid bare. If you want to understand how we got here (this mad world), this is the story. Living in London at the same time made this book resonate.
- Much like Jobs, even founders get fired. Its got the mess of Musk versus Altman in gory details. Geniuses also suffer from politics and stupidity it would seem.
