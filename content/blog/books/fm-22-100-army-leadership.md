The official leadership doctrine used in every U.S. Army officer training programme for over fifty years. Built around the core principle of "Be, Know, Do" — character first, then competence, then action — it covers leadership at every level from squad to strategic command, illustrated with historical vignettes from Marshall to Schwarzkopf. Surprisingly applicable well beyond the military.

## Observations

This one you can read one hundred times. Mac graduated from West Point in 1966. He was a two-tour Vietnam veteran, first as a Lieutenant and again as a Captain. As a Lieutenant Colonel teaching at Ft. Leavenworth, during what would ultimately be his final assignment, he rewrote the leadership manual, FM 22-100, for the United States Army. This was based in large part on lessons from Vietnam — bitter ones at that. Harris died on October 13, 1983, the same month the Army published the revised FM 22-100.

This book is as moral as it is historical. It is one of the most famous texts on leadership in the West. In 1985, West Point established the Boyd M. Harris Leadership Award, and in 2016 Mac was inducted into the Hall of Fame at Fort Leavenworth, KS. A trained psychologist, this is a text on human beings under pressure. It is a shame that he never lived longer to know that this book became legend. This text was a slap in the face to the Army post-Vietnam — no one wanted to know about this. It was a gift to future generations, as well as to us. Written by someone who was there, at the pointy end, and saw what failures looked like. Can you live up to this standard? Sure about that?

> The commander of a unit is responsible for all that his unit does, or fails to do.

Not often in the corporate world that you see anyone not slink away from the latter.
