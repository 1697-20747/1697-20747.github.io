Quality of Earnings

The bible of forensic accounting. How to read what companies are actually telling you, versus what they want you to think.
March 15, 2026 · 4 min

Published in 1987 and still required reading, this is a practical guide to interrogating corporate financial statements rather than accepting them at face value. O’Glove’s central argument is simple but important: reported earnings are a construction, and the gap between what a company tells its shareholders and what it tells the IRS is often where the truth lives. He walks through the mechanics of how earnings get inflated — accounting policy changes, aggressive revenue recognition, inventory manipulation, weak cash flow masked by strong net income — and gives you a repeatable framework for spotting it. The examples are dated but the logic is not, and in an era of adjusted EBITDA and non-GAAP everything, the core discipline feels more relevant than ever.

Observations#

Oldie but a goodie. Essential if you work in credit or equity analysis. Pairs well with Financial Shenanigans for a more modern treatment of the same problems. The chapter on the divergence between shareholder reporting and tax reporting alone is worth the price. The E in EBITDA is often just assumeded away (a lot more on the myths, uses and failing on EBITA elsewhere). This book try’s to unpick the healthy, from the unhealthy, for earnings. Intent matters here, if maximum reportable earnings is what you are chasing within accounting standards, there are some paths for that. As a lender or credit analyst, you are looking for long run reliable earnings. As laid out in George Orwell’s Animal Farm (1945), all earnings are equal for EBITDA, but some earnigns are more equal than others for lending purposes. This is the quintessential essance of credit analysis.

So, what kind of earnings are good? Earnigns that result in long run cash flow generation, that can service debt, pay for capex, etc, etc. Ideally earnings that closely track inflation. Refer Inflation blog, but this is important. Accounting is nomimal, it really only works for slow rates of change and fairly low inflation rates. Personally I think accounting standards should require a one page 10 year summary in real terms. That would sort the wheat from the chaff.

There is a fantastic 2016 Google Talk (wednesday, 29 June, 2016) with the man himself, so you can hear it straight. This should be mandatory training for credit analysts. The training, by the way, is how to think. Find it on youtube and really listen. Don’t think this book is dated, the concepts are not. They remain ageless. One of the all time top finance books. Plus the humility of the man is so evident. Buy the actual book. There is a good explanation in the talk about Berkhsire Hathaway.

Under credit anlysis, the core concepts in this book shall be applied to current and prior poster child credit cases. Again, and again and again. he refers to How to Lie with Statistics, a good book, and a reverse enginnering instruction manual for the fraudulently inclined.

When anyone presents you something, the presenter has the upper hand. Everyone has a bias. - Thornton L. O’Glove, 2016

Your job as critical thinker is twofold, firstly to know this is always the case, and secondly to have the clarity of thought and experience to be able to unpick it. What you will be presented is noise. What you want is the signal. Accounting fraud is purposeful. It is quite hard to unpick, which is the whole point.

Whatch the talk and listen. Everyone is in his cross haris. The Fed, the government. Its a wonderful and relentless attack. I love the invitation to Janet Yellen, with the sharp comment that perhaps she does not eat food. Which is a dig at stated verses real life inflation. The short but sharp Maddoff critique is devastating, including for pretend regulators, as well as those who lost their fortune. You can simply change the names for the current batch of problem cases, and most of the talk holds true. The man a legend in critical thinking, its a shame he is not more famous. But like many brilliant humble people, he is not much of a self promoter. 63k views on youtube since 2016. That is probably a lot less than one per analyst. Shame on you.
