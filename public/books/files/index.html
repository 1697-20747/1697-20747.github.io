Books

Books that shaped how I think — across economics, data, science, history, and beyond. A living list. Updated when something genuinely earns a spot. And yes, I have read them all, at least twice each.

Red Notice

Part financial thriller, part human rights story. Browder built one of the largest hedge funds in Russia, then watched Putin’s system destroy it. The backstory to the Magnitsky Act. Observations Removes the delusion that you can do long-term business with people you can’t trust. Character comes out in the end. Stay away. The descent of Russia back into the depths of a moral vacuum is truly sad. What might have been.

February 2, 2024 · 1 min · Bill Browder

SPQR

A history of Rome that focuses on how Rome worked as a society rather than just the emperors and battles. Beard is a brilliant classicist and a sharp writer. Observations We have not changed, have we? Self-importance, misogyny, self-interest, fraud, the pursuit of power, murder. Etc. Oh well — hygiene and medicine are better one supposes. Not many Cincinnatus in the world from then until now. Can’t see any of the current lot giving back power without the pre-thrashing election result tossing them out on their backside.

February 1, 2024 · 1 min · Mary Beard

Determined

Sapolsky’s follow-up makes the full case against free will. Provocative, rigorous, and uncomfortable. Read Behave first. Observations This is a very hard read. I don’t mean technically — I mean emotionally. The notes are the technical read. And yes, don’t skip them; read them all. It is a mini degree in neuroscience for the price of a book. I am still very torn on my position on this book. As of February 2026, the news broke of the conviction of a killer with profound mental health challenges — Deividas Skebas, 26, who stabbed Lilia Valutyte to death in a random attack outside the shop where her mother worked in Boston, Lincolnshire, on 28 July 2022. A jury at Lincoln Crown Court convicted him following an eight-day trial. There are multiple tragedies at play here: the young girl, the absence of mental health residential facilities, and a couple more I can think of. What a senseless waste. ...

January 31, 2024 · 1 min · Robert Sapolsky

Behave

Why humans do what they do — from the neuroscience of a single moment of behaviour back through hormones, evolution, and culture. The most comprehensive synthesis of human behaviour I’ve read. Observations A shocker. So almost everyone else has been focused on a game that I was unaware of, and did not care about: social status. This explains so much. There are many truths in here if you have worked at a large corporation. Or the government. The proxy of social-obsessed baboons as a model for humans is disturbing, while shockingly informative. ...

January 30, 2024 · 1 min · Robert Sapolsky

Life Is Simple

The story of Occam’s Razor and how the principle of simplicity drove scientific revolutions from Copernicus to Einstein to quantum mechanics. Beautifully written. Observations This is a masterclass in thinking and the foundation of science. Learn who William of Ockham was. Get on an aircraft and land where you were supposed to? Thank Johannes Kepler. The church was of the opinion that the sun revolved around the earth — it had to be simple, right? Churches cannot be wrong after all. Wrong. Thankfully Johannes chose not to oversimplify and fail. He chased that marginal error and dug out the truth. ...

January 29, 2024 · 2 min · Johnjoe McFadden

The Art of Doing Science and Engineering

Hamming spent decades at Bell Labs and asked himself: why do some people do great work and others don’t? This book is his answer. One of the most honest books about how science actually happens.

January 28, 2024 · 1 min · Richard Hamming

Brain of the Firm / Designing Freedom

The foundational texts behind Beer’s concept of POSIWID — the purpose of a system is what it does. A British cybernetician and management theorist, Beer argued that you cannot understand an organisation by reading its mission statement; you understand it by observing its outputs. Organisations, like all systems, do exactly what they are designed to do — even when that is the opposite of what they claim. Applies with uncomfortable precision to governments, corporations, and bureaucracies everywhere. ...

January 27, 2024 · 2 min · Stafford Beer

The Art of Statistics

How to think with data rather than be misled by it. Cambridge statistician Spiegelhalter walks through the essential principles of statistical reasoning using real-world cases — serial killers, cancer screening, hospital survival rates — to show where statistics illuminate and where they deceive. Less a technical manual than a guide to asking the right questions before trusting any number you are given. Observations This is a great book, as a read and as a reference text. If you are into machine learning, the concepts are essential. If you can find his London School of Economics lecture online it is definitely worth your time. I go back to this one quite often to help clarify my thinking. ...

January 26, 2024 · 1 min · David Spiegelhalter

Deep Learning

THE textbook. Rigorous mathematical treatment of neural networks from first principles. Not a tutorial — a foundation. Available free at deeplearningbook.org. Observations A wise woman once said: ‘Everyone thinks they are smart until they try doing Intragroup.’ For context, the Intragroup thing is a sack of spanners of complex regulation and data. This is another example, but one that is legitimately intellectually challenging. Read the book. It is quite possible that this book is the instruction manual for the rest of our lives as AI widens. Run through the parts that are of interest. Don’t copy the code — write it out, figure it out, change it, see what happens. Prepare for mental pain. This is not easy. But it is the underpinnings of AI. It will change how you think, which is the whole point. ...

January 25, 2024 · 2 min · Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio & Aaron Courville

Money: The Unauthorised Biography

A sweeping history of money from ancient clay tablets to modern finance. Ferguson is a fluent guide — opinionated, fast-moving, and never dull.
