The U.S. Army Leadership Field Manual (FM 22-100)

The official leadership doctrine used in every U.S. Army officer training programme for over fifty years. Built around the core principle of “Be, Know, Do” — character first, then competence, then action — it covers leadership at every level from squad to strategic command, illustrated with historical vignettes from Marshall to Schwarzkopf. Surprisingly applicable well beyond the military. Observations This one you can read one hundred times. Mac graduated from West Point in 1966. He was a two-tour Vietnam veteran, first as a Lieutenant and again as a Captain. As a Lieutenant Colonel teaching at Ft. Leavenworth, during what would ultimately be his final assignment, he rewrote the leadership manual, FM 22-100, for the United States Army. This was based in large part on lessons from Vietnam — bitter ones at that. Harris died on October 13, 1983, the same month the Army published the revised FM 22-100. ...

A Concise History of Warfare

Montgomery’s own account of warfare from ancient times to the mid-twentieth century, written with the authority of a man who commanded armies across North Africa and Northwest Europe. Clear-eyed on strategy, leadership, and the recurring mistakes that commanders make across centuries. Observations A nice summary of key battles, with a good tactical vs strategy examination. Key observation in literal sense from this book is that tactics, strategy and resources need to be in alignment to attain success. Everyone who ever worked on a large project should read this as many times as it takes to be understood. You cannot escape the constraint of resources. How many times in your life have you seen things fail because the resources — people, capital, time, technology — were materially diminished compared to the ask? Failure in such a situation is the fault of the person setting the strategy, not that of those executing. Strategy at the end of the day is the articulation of the bridge from means to the end. It reflects the painful, but necessary, choices made in the face of constraint. There are always constraints.

Once an Eagle

A sweeping military novel following two officers across decades of American wars. One of the finest studies of leadership, character, and moral compromise ever written. Required reading at several US war colleges for good reason. Observations If you have ever been in the army, or have suffered the usual tyranny in a large financial institution (yes large Banks, I do mean you), this one is for you. This is a military classic, that transcends the domain into life. Are you Sam? Really? There are no untainted heroes here. This is the life lesson. The title comes from the fable: “So in the Libyan fable it is told / That once an eagle, stricken with a dart, / Said, when he saw the fashion of the shaft, / ‘With our own feathers, not by others’ hands, / Are we now smitten’”. Read this book for a good history of war. Re-read it as many times as you need to until you understand the enemy is you. All of our carts bounce around in the rutted tracks of our deep rooted tendencies that we laid down long ago to get where we are.
