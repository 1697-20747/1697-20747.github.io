07 — Why Is the Correlation So Low? Key Findings

Six Structural Reasons the Aggregate Correlation is Near Zero

01 · SIGN REVERSAL ACROSS REGIMES

Advanced economies (Japan, Germany, Australia) show negative correlation: debt rises as spreads fall, driven by QE, safe-haven demand, and central bank credibility. Emerging markets (Indonesia, Argentina, Mexico) show positive correlation. These opposing effects cancel in aggregate.

02 · CURRENCY SOVEREIGNTY

Countries that borrow in their own currency (USA, UK, Japan) cannot technically default — the central bank can always monetise. This makes Debt/GDP a poor default-risk proxy. Japan exceeds 250% debt/GDP with near-zero credit spread. Argentina at ~80% faces double-digit spreads because it borrows in USD.

03 · MONETARY POLICY DOMINATES YIELDS

The 1970s–80s inflation cycle, the Volcker shock, and post-2008 QE compressed yields across all sovereigns regardless of fiscal position. The global interest rate cycle explains far more of yield variance than any single country's debt level (Bernanke & Gertler, 1995; King & Plosser, 1985).

04 · DEBT IS A SLOW VARIABLE; SPREADS ARE FAST

Debt/GDP changes by 2–5pp per year; spreads can move 100–500bps in weeks (e.g. Italy 2011, Argentina 2001, Turkey 2018). Spreads incorporate forward-looking risk; debt ratios are backward-looking accounting identities. This temporal mismatch reduces synchronous correlation.

05 · OMITTED VARIABLES

Primary balance trajectory, GDP growth, FX reserves, political risk, institutional quality (rule of law, central bank independence), external balance, and debt maturity structure all drive spreads independently of the gross debt stock. Reinhart, Rogoff & Savastano (2003) call this "debt intolerance" — threshold effects that vary by country history.

06 · RESERVE CURRENCY & GLOBAL SAFE HAVEN

US Treasuries are the global risk-free benchmark — their spread vs themselves is zero by construction. As US debt/GDP rose from 35% → 120%, demand for Treasuries also rose as the global economy grew. Germany's Bund plays a similar role in Europe. Reserve currency status severs the debt–spread link entirely (Gourinchas & Rey, 2007).
